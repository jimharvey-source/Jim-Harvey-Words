# Passing Clouds: the running order

Thirty-two poems. Twenty-nine from the archive, two from the older files, three (Travelling backwards on trains, Running towards the sun, The twat in the hat) sent in after the first read. She gave so much and Dreams came out to make room. Sasha Kissed Me and Solid stay in the order on the assumption their subjects say yes; if either says no, the poem comes out and nothing else moves.

## The lane, in one line

London, and the people who made me, told by a man who does not flinch and does not pose.

That line is for you, not the reader. It is the test for every new poem: does it have an address, a name, and no pose? If it does, it belongs. The reader never sees the line. They see the poems and work it out for themselves, which is the whole point.

## The shape

A prologue, four movements, a close. The movements go from the city to the people to the loss at the centre and out again into late love, then end on three poems that look at the mirror, the cigarette and the plane, and grin. The centre sits on its own with white space either side. Nothing funny goes next to it.

The order is for the site and for the book, if there is one. The newsletter order is different and is at the end.

## Prologue

1. The right words never come (with the Carver epigraph)

A man who talks for a living admitting the words fail when it matters. The right doorway. Modest, and it tells the reader what kind of poet this is.

## I. The city

2. Russell Square
3. Green Chair
4. Four Numbers in Soho
5. Text
6. I miss you
7. Birds, planes and hearts
8. Southampton Row
9. Dagenham Toilet

Eight London poems, each with a postcode. Russell Square first because it is eight lines and sets everything: a place, a bench, a woman who is not there. Green Chair second because it is the best. The movement ends on "Unsold", which turns the city from backdrop to subject.

## II. The people

10. The Tree
11. Solid
12. Grandad said
13. Til it made me sad to see flowers
14. Travelling backwards on trains
15. Running towards the sun
16. October Song

Four people, then the windbreak, then a dog, then the trees. The Tree and Solid are two Tonys, which is a coincidence the reader will enjoy. Grandad said is four lines and sits between Solid and the flowers poem so the reader is not braced for what the flowers poem does. Travelling backwards on trains follows: a poem about a father, then a poem about being one. Running towards the sun is a friend's dog, told from inside the dog, and the last death before the centre. October Song is the hinge: trees dying by degrees, "which it is that passed away". It carries the reader to the centre without announcing it.

## III. The centre

17. Night Time
18. Paper & Ink

On the site these two get their own page, with the section title and nothing else. In a book they get a blank page before and after. If you finish Post Script it goes between them. If not, two is right.

## IV. Late love

19. Travelling in hope
20. Fireworks
21. Sasha Kissed Me
22. Playlist
23. She is made of love
24. No Fucking Valentine (a sonnet)
25. A poem condemned to burn as a condition of its creation
26. A walk in the woods
27. Lists from a painful love remembered
28. She came for a season
29. In dreams remembered

Eleven poems that run as one affair, or all of them: hope, the spark, the kiss, the playlist, the sonnet, the burning, the footsteps separating, the lists, the season, and then dreams. It starts in Soho and ends in sleep. The order is the arc; a reader who reads them straight through gets a story without being told there is one.

## Close

30. The twat in the hat
31. Passing Clouds
32. If I should die today

Vanity, then mortality, then the grin. A man who cannot find a hat, a man choosing his smoke, a man on a plane. The title poem in the middle. The last word of the collection is "Yet." That is the right last word. It says the man is still here and still looking.

## The newsletter order

The newsletter does not follow the book. It follows the reader. The first eight sends have to make a stranger open the ninth, so they lead with place, wit and brevity, and hold the centre back until the reader knows who is talking.

Week 1: Russell Square. Week 2: If I should die today. Week 3: Green Chair. Week 4: Dagenham Toilet. Week 5: Four Numbers in Soho. Week 6: The Tree. Week 7: Text. Week 8: Passing Clouds.

Weeks 9 to 13: Travelling backwards on trains, Southampton Row, No Fucking Valentine, Lists, Birds, planes and hearts. Night Time goes out in week 14 or later, on its own, with a note from you that says less than you want it to.

Each send is one poem and three or four lines from you above it: where it was written, or what you were looking at. Not what it means. Never what it means.

## What this settles

The collection has a name, a shape and an order. Pile two (Primrose Hill, Humpback Whale, Love unspoken, Ben Hogan's Ghost, Printing Error and the rest) is a second collection or a second year of sends, not a reason to delay this one. The next step is the home: the domain, the site and the list.
